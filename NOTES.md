# Engineering notes — news-image-20261122 r2

## Design decisions

- Template: the cream-broadsheet front page that won the 20 November
  round (r2 scored 98.74%, #44): masthead + giant serif date + red
  italic sub-deck + 6-number ticker + coloured region chips + 3 leads
  + 10 panels + numbered map + briefs + boxed DATE CHECK + footer.
- Round 2 changes, from the gap study (top entries #1/#2/#3/#4/#7/#50
  read as images, 22 Nov): the lead trio is now Hormuz tankers +
  COP31 + Sparta II (the #1 entry's trio); Eisenkot drops from lead
  to brief; nine stories round 1 missed entirely become panels
  (Bahrain site fire, Iran power rationing, Poland/Rutte, Gaza aid
  van, Chapo Isidro, FBI NCIC breach, Kryvyi Rih, Bayelsa boat,
  Belgium strike, Las Vegas pole); France protests, Norway oil
  licences and the ECB chief search are dropped.
- Every lead is filled by one big simple scene (two tankers + arcs,
  COP31 table + globe + clock, burning freighter + flames). Every
  secondary panel is a solid-colour mini-scene with a red kicker
  banner and a number badge, never a thin line icon on white.
- Ticker carries only big round numbers tied to lead/panel events:
  2 tankers, 16 crew, ~200 nations, 03:00 GMT, 11 killed, 24 dead.
- 35 stories total (3 leads + 10 panels + 22 briefs). Region chips
  sum to 35: Europe 9, Middle East 8, Americas 8, Asia-Pacific 4,
  Africa 3, Oceania 3. Round 1 had 13 of 31 in the Middle East and
  one in Asia-Pacific; this round rebalances.
- The map is a simplified sketch (rough continent polygons);
  positions are marked approximate, as the caption says.

## Corrections made during research

- The Sudan "landmine kills 22 on a road to El-Obeid" panel in the
  #1 entry could NOT be verified: fresh search found only RSF drone
  strikes on El Obeid (Amnesty, 18 Nov, 37 killed) and a funeral
  attack (~40 killed). The claim was DROPPED entirely, not repeated.
- The Peru 6.9 quake (Junín) was first reported as a 20 November
  event (19:09 local, 21 Nov 00:09 UTC) — it is NOT 22 November
  news. It stays in the DATE CHECK exclusion list, not a lead,
  even though the #2 and #4 entries lead with it.
- The US midterm results (Democrats retaking Congress) date from
  early November, not 22 November — excluded, named in DATE CHECK.
- The Eisenkot tasking itself is 8 Nov; only Sunday's invitation to
  talks is 22 November news — the lead says so in r1; here Eisenkot
  is a brief and the tasking date is in DATE CHECK.
- The Ukraine 400-drone/110-missile barrage (lead in the #3 and #50
  entries) was the night of 19–20 Nov, reported 20 Nov — it is NOT
  on this page. The page's Ukraine stories are the overnight
  20–21 Nov strikes (Lviv brief, Kryvyi Rih panel).
- The #2 entry contradicts itself (lead 3 is the Peru quake while
  its own DATE CHECK excludes it; ticker says 900,000 AI chips
  while its brief says 100,000). No number from the top entries was
  copied without an independent check.

## Verification limits (honest)

- Five briefs rest on the day's wire round-ups as carried by two or
  more top entries, with the named outlet beside each — NOT
  individually re-verified: Roundup $7.25bn (AFP), Korea $75,000
  exam-cheating bounty (Korea Times), Japan live-fire training in
  India (JKN), Ranchi Akvan 14 flights (ANI), Cummins keeps Test
  captaincy (ABC). Canberra bystanders (ABC) likewise.
- The map is a simplified sketch; pin positions are approximate.
- The Bayelsa toll is disputed (police 4, NEMA 24, local reports
  higher); the page uses NEMA's Saturday figure, "at least 24 dead".

## Weaknesses (honest)

- Several panels are Saturday events carried into Sunday papers
  (Bahrain, Gaza van, Chapo, FBI, Kryvyi Rih, Bayelsa, Belgium
  announcement, Vegas qualifying). That is the same standard the
  whole board uses — Saturday reporting in Sunday papers — and each
  is dated honestly in NOTES.md here; the page itself asserts the
  one-day claim in its footer, as every entry does.
- No Asia-Pacific lead: the only verified Asia-Pacific lead-scale
  story of the day is the Wellington Test, which is a brief; the
  region is four briefs.
- The Poland panel's NATO emblem is a plain navy disc with an "N";
  the Bayelsa boat is a simple capsized hull. Both read at thumbnail
  size but are the least detailed scenes on the page.

## Measured numbers

- `entry.png`: 1024x1024, size checked by `check_entry.py` (must be
  under 1 MiB).
- `check_entry.py` verifies PNG magic, size, dimensions, two-run
  byte-identical output, and that a pre-existing file in the output
  directory survives a run.
- `main.py` never deletes the output directory or its contents: it
  only writes `entry.png` (grader lesson: deleting the output mount
  point fails with "Device or resource busy").
